MITI Battery AP Flow

Last updated: 2026-05-27 — refreshed to the 1 Jan 2026 battery importation SOP + Customs Ruling (Classification Letter) process
Purpose: This guide covers the MITI Approved Permit (AP) for new reusable batteries / accumulators related to electric scooters or motor vehicles, under the battery importation SOP effective 1 January 2026.
Core rule (effective 1 Jan 2026): The previous manual No Objection Letter route is replaced by an AP applied through the ePermit system. The AP applies to new reusable batteries (accumulators) for motor vehicles classified under HS headings 87.01, 87.02, 87.03, 87.04, 87.05, 87.09 and 87.11 — electric scooters typically fall under 87.03 or 87.11, so scooter batteries imported under these headings are in scope. The AP package now requires a Classification Letter from Customs (a Customs Ruling — see Section 6) plus a Technical Evaluation Report. If the battery is inside a complete scooter (CBU), the main route may be the MITI Micromobility AP, but the same battery documents are still needed.

2. First Pre-Check

  • Confirm whether the battery is imported inside a complete scooter (CBU) or separately.
  • Confirm the battery's HS heading with your forwarder/customs agent; do not reuse the scooter HS code automatically. If in doubt, lock it in with a Customs Ruling (Section 6).
  • Register the company on ePermit (newepermit.dagangnet.com.my) early — account setup takes time.
  • Start the Customs Classification Letter (Customs Ruling) early — it is a required AP document and is issued by RMCD, not MITI.
  • Collect the Technical Evaluation Report inputs from the supplier: UN 38.3, UN R100/R136 (as applicable) and the relevant IEC test report. Keep SDS/MSDS for shipping/logistics regardless.

4. Document Package

Five core documents required by the SOP:
1. Classification Letter from Customs (Customs Ruling — see Section 6) 2. Application Letter (on company letterhead) 3. Product Catalogue 4. Invoice 5. Technical Evaluation Report (TER)
The Technical Evaluation Report (TER) must comprise:
- UN 38.3 (lithium battery transport test summary/report) - UN R100 (four tyres) OR UN R136 (two tyres), as applicable - Relevant IEC test report (e.g. IEC 62133 / MS IEC 62133)
Supporting documents to keep on hand (commonly requested / needed for clearance & logistics):
- Company profile / SSM, importer details - Supplier / manufacturer details - Battery specification sheet, model number, voltage / capacity / Wh rating - Packing list, country of origin - SDS / MSDS (dangerous-goods handling) - ePermit / company AP registration
Note: A complete application is processed within five (5) working days (excluding the submission date). Incomplete documents reset the clock — get the Classification Letter and full TER ready before submitting.

5. Quick Flow

1. Confirm whether the battery is imported separately or inside a complete scooter, and its HS heading (87.01–87.11). 2. Apply for the Customs Classification Letter (Customs Ruling) from RMCD — see Section 6. 3. Collect supplier documents and assemble the Technical Evaluation Report: UN 38.3 + UN R100/R136 (as applicable) + relevant IEC report. 4. Register / log in to ePermit (newepermit.dagangnet.com.my). 5. Submit the Battery AP with the 5 core documents (Classification Letter, Application Letter, Product Catalogue, Invoice, TER). 6. MITI processes a complete application within 5 working days. 7. Use the approved AP for customs clearance.

6. How to get the Customs Classification Letter (Customs Ruling)

What it is: A Customs Ruling is a written, binding tariff-classification decision issued by the RMCD Technical Services Division, Classification, Tariff & Drafting Branch. It is the "Classification Letter from Customs" the MITI Battery AP requires (document #1 in Section 4), and it gives you certainty on the battery's HS heading before importing.

Validity: A Customs Ruling on Origin is valid for 3 years and is non-renewable (a new application is needed after). A classification ruling remains valid unless the law, the tariff (HS) code, or the product facts change — re-apply if any of these change.
